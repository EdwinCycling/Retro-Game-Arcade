# 📚 Games Catalog & Historical Dossiers

A detailed reference for all 35 authentic historic game recreations included in the **Classic Retro Arcade Vault**.

---

## 1. Pong (1972)
- **Developer**: Allan Alcorn / Atari
- **Platform**: Arcade (Discrete Transistor-Transistor Logic, no CPU)
- **Historical Significance**: The legendary coin-operated title that launched the commercial video game industry.
- **Engine Highlights**: Precise paddle-ball vector deflection depending on collision distance from paddle center, scanline CRT rendering, and authentic square-wave audio bleeps.

## 2. Space Invaders (1978)
- **Developer**: Tomohiro Nishikado / Taito
- **Platform**: Intel 8080 Arcade
- **Historical Significance**: Caused a legendary shortage of 100-yen coins in Japan; introduced curved bunker destruction and accelerating enemy march tempo.
- **Engine Highlights**: Authentic 55-alien grid with shifting columns, Mystery Flying Saucer (worth 50 to 300 pts), 4 destructible bunkers, and mobile gyroscope tilt steering.

## 3. Asteroids (1979)
- **Developer**: Lyle Rains & Ed Logg / Atari
- **Platform**: Atari Vector Display Generator (DVG)
- **Historical Significance**: Atari’s highest-selling arcade game of all time, utilizing bright vector cathode ray lines rather than raster pixels.
- **Engine Highlights**: True Newtonian inertia, screen wrap-around, hyperspace jump risks, splitting rock physics, and authentic vector glow shaders.

## 4. Pac-Man (1980)
- **Developer**: Toru Iwatani / Namco
- **Platform**: Zilog Z80 Arcade
- **Historical Significance**: The pop-culture phenomenon that redefined character-driven arcade gaming.
- **Engine Highlights**: Authentic recreations of all 4 ghost AI algorithms:
  - **Blinky (Red)**: Direct tile chaser (Cruise Elroy acceleration mode).
  - **Pinky (Pink)**: Ambusher targeting 4 tiles ahead of Pac-Man.
  - **Inky (Cyan)**: Flanker calculating vectors between Blinky and Pac-Man.
  - **Clyde (Orange)**: Coward fleeing to bottom-left corner when within 8 tiles.

## 5. Donkey Kong (1981)
- **Developer**: Shigeru Miyamoto / Nintendo
- **Platform**: Z80 Arcade
- **Historical Significance**: The debut of Jumpman (later Mario), Pauline, and Donkey Kong; pioneered narrative platforming.
- **Engine Highlights**: Sloped girder collision detection, rolling barrel physics, climbable ladders, destructive hammer power-ups, and 100m rivet pulling.

## 6. Frogger (1981)
- **Developer**: Konami / Sega
- **Platform**: Z80 Arcade
- **Historical Significance**: Iconic road and river crossing timer game.
- **Engine Highlights**: Five home bays with bonus flies, floating log speed variations, sinking turtles, and snappy swipe gesture controls.

## 7. 3D Monster Maze (1981)
- **Developer**: Malcolm Evans / J.K. Greye Software
- **Platform**: Sinclair ZX81 (16KB RAM)
- **Historical Significance**: The world’s first 3D survival horror game on a home microcomputer.
- **Engine Highlights**: Low-resolution pseudo-3D perspective maze with heartbeat distance alerts ("REX HAS SEEN YOU", "RUN! HE IS BEHIND YOU!").

## 8. Arcadians (1982)
- **Developer**: Orlando / Acornsoft
- **Platform**: BBC Micro (6502 CPU)
- **Historical Significance**: The premier arcade shooter on the British BBC Micro system.
- **Engine Highlights**: High-speed Mode 1 multi-colored alien formations with diving dive-bomb attacks and synthesized SN76489 chiptune sound effects.

## 9. Rocket Raid (1982)
- **Developer**: Jonathan Griffiths / Acornsoft
- **Platform**: BBC Micro & Acorn Electron
- **Historical Significance**: The seminal 6502 horizontal-scrolling cave shooter inspired by Konami's *Scramble*.
- **Engine Highlights**: 5 unique terrain sections, fuel depot bombing runs, rocket ground launches, and authentic Acorn monochrome gray palette.

## 10. Q*bert (1982)
- **Developer**: Warren Davis & Jeff Lee / Gottlieb
- **Platform**: Arcade
- **Historical Significance**: Celebrated isometric puzzle hopper with swearing speech bubble synthesis (`@!#?@!`).
- **Engine Highlights**: 28-cube isometric pyramid, color cycle state machine, Coily the Snake tracking AI, and flying escape discs.

## 11. Demon Attack (1982)
- **Developer**: Rob Fulop / Imagic
- **Platform**: Atari 2600 & TI-99/4A
- **Historical Significance**: One of the most visually stunning titles of the early 80s home console generation.
- **Engine Highlights**: Splitting alien demons, descending laser attacks, smooth analog base steering, and iPhone tilt gyroscope support.

## 12. Zaxxon (1982)
- **Developer**: Ikegami Tsushinki / Sega
- **Platform**: Z80 Arcade
- **Historical Significance**: The world's first isometric 3D scrolling shooter featuring altitude perception.
- **Engine Highlights**: Axonometric 2:1 projection, real-time floor altitude shadow tracking, fuel tanks, force field gates, and space fortress dogfights.

## 13. Chuckie Egg (1983)
- **Developer**: Nigel Alderton / A&F Software
- **Platform**: ZX Spectrum & BBC Micro
- **Historical Significance**: Legendary British micro platformer famous for blistering 50Hz running speed.
- **Engine Highlights**: Hen-House Harry physics, 8 floors of golden eggs, grain seed bonuses, fast elevators, and the caged giant duck timer.

## 14. Mario Bros. (1983)
- **Developer**: Shigeru Miyamoto / Nintendo
- **Platform**: Z80 Arcade
- **Historical Significance**: Introduced Luigi, green sewer pipes, and the floor-bumping flip mechanic.
- **Engine Highlights**: Floor collision bumping from below, Shellcreepers, Sidesteppers (must be bumped twice), and the central POW block.

## 15. Manic Miner (1983)
- **Developer**: Matthew Smith / Bug-Byte Software
- **Platform**: Sinclair ZX Spectrum (48K)
- **Historical Significance**: The foundational British platform game with surreal humor and memorable cavern rooms.
- **Engine Highlights**: 20 distinct cavern rooms (Central Cavern, Cold Room, Menagerie), pixel-perfect jump parabolas, color-clash mitigation, and oxygen depletion.

## 16. FRAK! (1984)
- **Developer**: Orlando / Aardvark Software
- **Platform**: BBC Micro (6502)
- **Historical Significance**: Renowned for its physics-based yoyo weapon and Troglodyte screams when falling off edges.
- **Engine Highlights**: Inertial bouncing rope/yoyo mechanics, scrubby monsters, and the famous synthesized speech curse.

## 17. Tetris (1984)
- **Developer**: Alexey Pajitnov / Electronika 60
- **Platform**: Electronika 60 / IBM PC
- **Historical Significance**: The greatest puzzle game of all time, created at the Soviet Academy of Sciences.
- **Engine Highlights**: 7 official tetrominoes (I, J, L, O, S, T, Z), Super Rotation System (SRS), ghost projection, level gravity ramp, and line clear scoring.

## 18. King's Quest I: Quest for the Crown (1984)
- **Developer**: Roberta Williams / Sierra On-Line
- **Platform**: IBM PC / PCjr (AGI Engine)
- **Historical Significance**: The groundbreaking graphical adventure game that introduced 3D perspective depth and interactive text parser puzzles.
- **Engine Highlights**: Sierra AGI parser with dual English/Dutch vocabulary, inventory puzzles (Magic Mirror, Chest of Gold, Shield of Achilles), and full story state saving.

## 19. Repton (1985)
- **Developer**: Tim Tyler / Superior Software
- **Platform**: BBC Micro & Acorn Electron
- **Historical Significance**: Superior Software's smash hit with over 125,000 copies sold in the UK.
- **Engine Highlights**: Earth digging mechanics, falling rock gravity & cascades, diamond collection, time bombs, keys & cages, and all 12 original level passwords (A-L).

## 20. Eindeloos (1985)
- **Developer**: Radarsoft
- **Platform**: Commodore 64 & MSX
- **Historical Significance**: Dutch video game milestone featuring an endless desert scouting mission.
- **Engine Highlights**: 360-degree helicopter rotation, fuel consumption, supply depot landings, and real-time scanning radar.

## 21. Super Mario Bros. (1985)
- **Developer**: Shigeru Miyamoto / Nintendo
- **Platform**: Nintendo Entertainment System (NES)
- **Historical Significance**: The definitive side-scroller that rescued the video game industry after the 1983 crash.
- **Engine Highlights**: World 1-1 to 1-4, smooth horizontal scrolling, Super Mushroom growth, Fire Flowers, Goombas, Koopa Troopas, and Bowser castle bridge axes.

## 22. Space Quest I: The Sarien Encounter (1986)
- **Developer**: Mark Crowe & Scott Murphy / Sierra On-Line
- **Platform**: IBM PC MS-DOS (EGA 16-Color)
- **Historical Significance**: Hilarious sci-fi adventure following Roger Wilco, cosmic janitor aboard the Arcada.
- **Engine Highlights**: Interactive text parser, inventory puzzles (Cartridge, Keycard, Gadget), Sarien droid evasion, escape pod launch, and full dual English/Dutch language support.

## 23. OutRun (1986)
- **Developer**: Yu Suzuki / Sega
- **Platform**: Sega OutRun (Super Scaler Arcade)
- **Historical Significance**: Pioneered non-linear road branching, force-feedback steering, and selectable in-game FM radio music.
- **Engine Highlights**: Pseudo-3D road curve & elevation projection, red Ferrari Testarossa Spider, traffic cars, high/low gear shifting up to 293 km/h, and real-time FM synthesis of *Magical Sound Shower*, *Passing Breeze*, and *Splash Wave*.

## 24. Double Dragon (1987)
- **Developer**: Yoshihisa Kishimoto / Technos Japan
- **Platform**: Arcade
- **Historical Significance**: The undisputed king of cooperative street brawlers.
- **Engine Highlights**: 2.5D depth movement, punches, kicks, jump kicks, back-elbow strikes, weapon pickups (baseball bats, oil drums), and Shadow Boss encounters.

## 25. Battle Chess (1988)
- **Developer**: Brian Fargo / Interplay
- **Platform**: Amiga 500 & MS-DOS
- **Historical Significance**: Turned the classic game of chess into a humorous battle royale with unique kill animations for every piece pairing.
- **Engine Highlights**: Full standard chess rule engine (castling, en passant, pawn promotion) with procedural medieval duel sequences.

## 26. Exile (1988)
- **Developer**: Peter Irvin & Jeremy Smith / Superior Software
- **Platform**: BBC Micro Mode 5 (6502 Assembly)
- **Historical Significance**: Recognized by modern game historians as the world's first Metroidvania physics sandbox and procedural physics engine.
- **Engine Highlights**: Newtonian gravitational physics, inertia, wind tunnels, Mike Finn jetpack thrusters, particle blaster, teleporter, and gravity-defying boulder carrying.

## 27. Prince of Persia (1989)
- **Developer**: Jordan Mechner / Brøderbund
- **Platform**: Apple II / MS-DOS
- **Historical Significance**: Revolutionized realistic character animation through manual rotoscoping from VHS footage.
- **Engine Highlights**: Momentum-based ledge grabbing, running long jumps, floor spikes, loose flagstones, and timing-based sword fencing.

## 28. 3D Pinball Power (1989)
- **Developer**: Mastertronic
- **Platform**: Commodore 64
- **Historical Significance**: Fast-paced Commodore 64 pinball simulation.
- **Engine Highlights**: Sub-step physics calculation, flipper impulses, slingshot bumpers, bonus spinners, multi-ball scoring, and pinball tilt/nudge mechanics.

## 29. Lemmings (1991)
- **Developer**: David Jones & Mike Dailly / DMA Design (Psygnosis)
- **Platform**: Commodore Amiga & C64
- **Historical Significance**: Iconic puzzle strategy game giving birth to DMA Design (who later created *Grand Theft Auto*).
- **Engine Highlights**: 100 individual walking lemming entities, destructible voxel terrain, Bashers, Miners, Diggers, Blockers, Builders, and the 5-second "Oh No!" explosion timer.

## 30. Wolfenstein 3D (1992)
- **Developer**: John Carmack & John Romero / id Software
- **Platform**: MS-DOS (VGA 320x200)
- **Historical Significance**: The "Grandfather of 3D First-Person Shooters".
- **Engine Highlights**: DDA (Digital Differential Analyzer) raycasting algorithm, textured 64x64 wall slices, Nazi guards, secret pushwalls, and Gatling gun fire.

## 31. DOOM (1993)
- **Developer**: id Software
- **Platform**: MS-DOS
- **Historical Significance**: A gaming landmark that brought networked multiplayer deathmatches and 3D modding into popular culture.
- **Engine Highlights**: High-speed raycasting with variable sector heights, Imps, Shotguns, Chainguns, pixelated muzzle flashes, and E1M1 Hangar layout.

## 32. Duke Nukem 3D (1996)
- **Developer**: 3D Realms / Ken Silverman (Build Engine)
- **Platform**: MS-DOS
- **Historical Significance**: Known for its interactive environments, bathroom humor, and iconic Hollywood action one-liners.
- **Engine Highlights**: High-resolution WebGL 3D rendering, interactive light switches, flying Pigcops, mighty boot kicks, and pistol ballistics.

## 33. Nokia Snake (1997)
- **Developer**: Taneli Armanto / Nokia
- **Platform**: Nokia 6110 Mobile Phone
- **Historical Significance**: Preloaded on hundreds of millions of Nokia phones; introduced mobile gaming to the world.
- **Engine Highlights**: Pixel-perfect 84x48 monochrome green LCD display, grid-based snake growth, 9 selectable speed levels, and authentic 1-bit monotone beeps.

## 34. Half-Life (1998)
- **Developer**: Gabe Newell & Marc Laidlaw / Valve
- **Platform**: PC Windows (GoldSrc Engine)
- **Historical Significance**: Pioneered seamless in-game narrative storytelling without cutscenes.
- **Engine Highlights**: 3D WebGL Black Mesa research facility, Gordon Freeman's HEV suit, signature Red Crowbar melee, MP5 submachine gun, and parasitic Headcrabs.

## 35. Temple Run 3D (2011)
- **Developer**: Keith Shepherd & Natalia Luckyanova / Imangi Studios
- **Platform**: iOS & Android
- **Historical Significance**: The pioneer of the modern mobile endless 3D runner genre.
- **Engine Highlights**: Procedural 3D WebGL temple pathways, 90-degree fast corner turns, obstacle jumping & sliding, tilt-leaning coin magnets, and Demon Monkey pursuit.

## 36. Game Boy DMG-01 Console & Game Vault (1989)
- **Developer**: Gunpei Yokoi & Nintendo R&D1
- **Platform**: Game Boy Handheld (Sharp LR35902 8-bit CPU @ 4.19 MHz)
- **Historical Significance**: The revolutionary handheld console that popularized portable gaming worldwide with over 118 million units sold.
- **Hardware & Engine Highlights**: 160×144 dot-matrix LCD screen with authentic 4-shade olive green palettes, contrast slider wheel, physical cartridge insertion mechanism, and 10 iconic games playable in the vault:
  1. **Super Mario Land (1989)**: Sarasaland adventure with Birabuto kingdom, Sky Pop airplane, and Tatanga spaceship boss.
  2. **Tetris DMG (1989)**: The world-famous puzzle game bundled with the Game Boy, featuring the iconic *Korobeiniki* chiptune soundtrack.
  3. **Dr. Mario (1990)**: Fast-paced virus eradication puzzle game with falling double-colored vitamin capsules.
  4. **Metroid II: Return of Samus (1991)**: Atmospheric bounty hunting on planet SR388 hunting 39 Metroid evolutions.
  5. **Kirby's Dream Land (1992)**: Masahiro Sakurai's debut featuring Kirby's inhale, float, and star-spitting mechanics against King Dedede.
  6. **Super Mario Land 2: 6 Golden Coins (1992)**: Expanded Mario platformer introducing Mario's greedy rival Wario.
  7. **The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening (1993)**: Koholint Island mystery adventure featuring the Wind Fish and 8 Instrument dungeons.
  8. **Donkey Kong '94 (1994)**: Masterpiece puzzle platformer expanding the original arcade classic with 101 puzzle levels and Mario backflips.
  9. **Pokémon Red & Blue (1996)**: Satoshi Tajiri's revolutionary monster collecting RPG featuring 151 Pokémon across Kanto.
  10. **Wario Land II (1998)**: Innovative puzzle platformer starring the immortal anti-hero Wario with reaction transformations (Flat Wario, Hot Wario, Zombie Wario).

## 37. PlayStation 1 (PS1 32-Bit) Console & Discs (1994)
- **Developer**: Ken Kutaragi / Sony Computer Entertainment
- **Platform**: Sony PlayStation 1 (LSI R3000A 32-Bit RISC CPU @ 33.8688 MHz)
- **Historical Significance**: The console that ushered video games into the 3D polygon and double-speed CD-ROM disc era, selling over 102 million systems.
- **Hardware & Engine Highlights**: 320×240 textured 3D mesh rendering, 24-channel SPU CD audio, Memory Card saving, DualShock analog vibration controllers, and 3D games:
  1. **Crash Bandicoot 3D (1996)**: Naughty Dog's landmark 3D corridor platformer on N. Sanity Island featuring spin attacks, Wumpa fruit, TNT crates, and Aku Aku tiki mask protection.
  2. **Ridge Racer 3D (1994)**: Namco's iconic launch 3D drift racer featuring high-speed 60 FPS drifting around the Mountain Cliff Circuit and Namco's energetic CD techno score.

## 38. Game Boy Advance SP (GBA SP 32-Bit) Console & Cartridges (2003)
- **Developer**: Nintendo R&D2 / Satoru Iwata
- **Platform**: Game Boy Advance SP (ARM7TDMI 32-Bit RISC CPU @ 16.78 MHz)
- **Historical Significance**: The iconic foldable clamshell handheld with a frontlit/backlit screen (AGS-001 / AGS-101) and rechargeable Li-Ion battery.
- **Hardware & Engine Highlights**: 240×160 32-bit color screen with 32,768 colors, 6 metallic shell colorways (Silver, Cobalt Blue, Flame Red, Onyx Black, Pearl Pink, Tribal), DirectSound 6-channel stereo PCM audio, and flagship 32-bit games:
  1. **Super Mario Advance 4: Super Mario Bros. 3 (2003)**: The definitive 32-bit remaster of SMB3 featuring Tanooki suit flying, Frog suits, digitized Charles Martinet voice clips, and Koopaling airships.
  2. **Pokémon Emerald (2004)**: The ultimate Generation III Hoenn adventure featuring animated 32-bit battle sprites, Team Magma vs. Team Aqua storyline, summoning Rayquaza at Sootopolis City, and the Battle Frontier.
  3. **The Legend of Zelda: The Minish Cap (2004)**: Capcom/Flagship's pixel-art action adventure starring Link and the talking cap Ezlo, featuring size-shifting to explore Hyrule as a microscopic Minish and forging the Four Sword.
